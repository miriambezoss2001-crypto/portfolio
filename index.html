System overview

The project combines material extraction, transportation and vessel loading into an integrated bulk material handling chain. The portfolio focuses on the engineering interfaces between mechanical equipment, electrical drives, automation, instrumentation and commissioning activities.

Control architecture

At a conceptual level, operator commands and plant-level communication reach the PLC. The PLC coordinates drive commands and sequence logic, while feedback from encoders, sensors and safety devices is used to determine status, interlocks and permitted movements.

Reclaimer

The portal scraper reclaimer uses travelling drives, scraper chains, boom luffing, cable reel and lubrication systems. Operating modes include maintenance, cabin, automatic and program modes. Automatic operation depends on a valid program, remote local-control stations and machine readiness.

During automatic reclaiming, chain activation is followed by controlled travelling. Travel speed can be managed manually or through a PID-based option, while boom position is monitored through encoder feedback. The PLC also manages programmed working zones and position memory so the machine can resume after a controlled stop when conditions permit.

Shiploader

The shiploader integrates travelling, slewing, boom luffing, conveyors, telescopic chute, cable reel and auxiliary systems. In automatic mode, conveyor start-up follows a downstream-to-upstream sequence, while stopping proceeds upstream-to-downstream once material has cleared the relevant conveyor.

Movement functions use operator controls and interlocks. Examples include rail-clamp status for travelling, lock-pin status for slewing, limit switches and feedback for boom luffing, and cable-reel conditions linked to travelling.

Automation & HMI

The documented control philosophy includes mode selection, start-order handling, sequence logic, alarm registers, drive status, working-zone parameters, speed setpoints, encoder feedback, communication status and maintenance information. HMI screens provide operators with machine status and the commands or settings relevant to each subsystem.

The portfolio describes the documented machine functionality; it does not claim personal authorship of PLC software or proprietary control code.

Safety & interlocks

Safety devices are integrated into the control philosophy to prevent unsafe movement or stop relevant drives. The documentation distinguishes between instruments whose signals are analysed by the PLC and hardwired safety functions that act through safety relays or the drive power circuit.

Technical deep dive — reclaimer

Operating modes

No Mode, Maintenance, Cabin, Automatic and Program. Automatic operation requires a valid program and all local control stations in Remote.

Drive & feedback

Six travelling drives are controlled by VFDs. Encoder feedback provides position information; travel speed can be handled manually or through a PID-based option.

Automatic sequence

Horn → main chains → auxiliary chain when required → travelling after the documented delay, with programmed working zones and position memory.

Technical deep dive — shiploader

Conveyor sequence

Automatic start proceeds downstream-to-upstream, advancing when the previous conveyor reaches full speed. Stop proceeds upstream-to-downstream after the conveyor is empty.

Positioning

Travelling, slewing and boom luffing use operator controls with limit, lock and feedback conditions governing permitted movement.

Material discharge

The telescopic chute provides vertical positioning and trimming-spot control, with load, rope, vibration and emergency-limit protection.

Windar Renovables

Robotic blasting & painting

Windar was implementing robotic blasting and painting systems for large components. My work included preparing technical reports covering installation, equipment arrangement, process stages and safety behaviour.

System layout

Documentation considered the supporting structure, rail arrangement, wall clearances, levelling requirements and smoothness of the supporting surface, together with hoppers and other required installation elements.

Safety behaviour

The detection system stopped the robot when a person entered the protected area. A projection-system fault generated an alarm and interrupted the process; after restart, the system resumed from the stopping point.

Process analysis & automation justification

I prepared a spreadsheet comparison between manual and robotic operation to quantify the operational factors relevant to the investment decision.

Load time

Compared the time required from process start to completion for manual and robotic operation.

Labour

Compared number of operators and working hours associated with each process configuration.

Material use

Compared material consumption per treated unit area as part of the process evaluation.

The internship report states that the robotic installation represented a significant initial investment and could reduce process time and resource consumption; exact internal figures are not reproduced.

Workshop equipment troubleshooting

I investigated a lifting system used to position tower sections for welding. The equipment combined hydraulic cylinders with a lifting table; one of two comparable machines became blocked during operation.

  • Compared the functioning and faulty machines and reviewed their technical drawings.
  • Extracted table and cylinder stroke information and compared it with the cylinder manufacturer's catalogue data.
  • Identified the relationship between available cylinder stroke and the required lifting position.
  • The subsequent solution incorporated sensors to prevent the table from exceeding its intended position.

Final Degree Project

Research question

The study examined how exposure to a high-humidity environment affects the fracture and delamination behaviour of adhesive joints in unidirectional carbon-fibre/epoxy composites, with particular focus on Mode I opening fracture.

Material

Unidirectional carbon fibre/epoxy composite with an epoxy-based structural adhesive joint.

Ageing

Three conditions: unaged reference, one week and four weeks of accelerated environmental exposure.

Response

Interlaminar fracture toughness, fatigue crack growth and energy-release-rate behaviour.

Experimental workflow

The work combined specimen preparation, environmental conditioning, mechanical testing and data reduction.

01Fabrication and preparation of DCB specimens
02Accelerated environmental ageing in a climatic chamber
03Static Mode I testing to characterise fracture toughness
04Dynamic testing and crack-growth analysis

Fracture mechanics & data reduction

The analysis used compliance and load-displacement data to determine the energy release rate associated with crack propagation. The project applied Modified Beam Theory, Compliance Calibration and Modified Compliance Calibration approaches.

  • GIC: Mode I interlaminar fracture toughness / critical energy release rate.
  • da/dN: crack-growth rate under cyclic loading.
  • ΔG: cyclic variation in energy release rate.
  • Excel-based data processing organised specimen inputs, cycles, crack length, loads and displacements and calculated derived parameters.

Fatigue & crack-growth modelling

Crack propagation was analysed through a Paris-law-type relationship adapted to composite delamination. The project evaluated the relationship between crack-growth rate and the relevant energy-release parameter and obtained regression constants from the experimental data.

The portfolio does not reproduce numerical results from the thesis; it focuses on the engineering methodology and analytical workflow.

Standards & methods

ASTM D5528ASTM D6115-97ISO 15024:2023Modified Beam TheoryCompliance CalibrationModified Compliance CalibrationParis-law analysis

Engineering outcome

The analysis showed that increasing environmental exposure was associated with lower resistance to crack propagation: the crack-growth behaviour shifted toward lower energy-release levels for comparable growth rates.
